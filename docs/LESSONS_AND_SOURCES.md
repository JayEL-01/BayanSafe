# BayanSafe: Disaster Preparedness Lessons & Official Philippine Sources

This document details the official Philippine disaster preparedness guidelines that support each scenario and educational lesson in BayanSafe. All safety procedures reflect official materials published by Philippine government agencies.

---

## 1. National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council (NDRRMC)
- **Primary Reference:** *National Disaster Response Plan (NDRP), 2024*
- **Role in Game:** Forms the core protocol for inter-agency disaster response, incident command coordination, evacuation center operations, and prioritizing life safety over property.
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Standardized incident reporting and community-level disaster risk reduction (BDRRMC).
  - Pre-emptive and forced evacuation protocols during impending severe events.
  - Relief distribution, accounting for displaced populations, and vulnerable sector prioritization (children, elderly, pregnant women, persons with disabilities).

## 2. Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration (PAGASA)

### A. Tropical Cyclone Wind Signals (TCWS)
- **Levels & Lead Times:**
  - **Signal No. 1:** 39–61 km/h wind threat; 36 hours lead time. Early preparation, securing loose outdoor items.
  - **Signal No. 2:** 62–88 km/h wind threat; 24 hours lead time. Children, elderly, and vulnerable residents prepare for evacuation.
  - **Signal No. 3:** 89–117 km/h wind threat; 18 hours lead time. Significant structural danger to light buildings; mandatory movement to evacuation centers.
  - **Signal No. 4:** 118–184 km/h wind threat; 12 hours lead time. Severe damage expected.
  - **Signal No. 5:** ≥ 185 km/h wind threat; 12 hours lead time. Extreme catastrophic damage.
- **Rule in Game:** Never risk lives to retrieve fishing boats, crops, or livestock when warnings are raised. Evacuate to designated sturdy shelters before storm onset.

### B. Heavy Rainfall & Flood Warning System
- **Monitoring (Yellow / Alert):** 7.5–15 mm/h rainfall. Flooding possible in low-lying areas. Monitor weather updates.
- **Flood Warning (Orange / Warning):** 15–30 mm/h rainfall. Flooding threatening. Prepare to evacuate.
- **Severe Flood Warning (Red / Evacuation):** > 30 mm/h rainfall or serious flooding occurring. Evacuate immediately!
- **Flood Safety Rules:**
  - *Turn Around, Don't Drown:* Never walk, swim, or drive through moving floodwater. 15 cm of moving water can knock down an adult; 30–60 cm can sweep away vehicles.
  - Electrical Isolation: Submerged outlets, damaged transformers, and fallen wires are lethal electrical hazards. Report to authorities immediately.
  - Move to designated higher ground before escape routes become submerged.

### C. Storm Surge Guidance
- Coastal settlements must evacuate immediately inland and to high ground upon storm surge warnings; do not wait along shorelines.

## 3. Philippine Institute of Volcanology and Seismology (PHIVOLCS)

### A. Earthquake Safety (2025 Davao Oriental Earthquake Primer & National Drills)
- **During Strong Shaking:**
  - **Drop, Cover, and Hold On:** Drop down to the floor, take cover beneath a sturdy desk or table, protect head and neck, and hold on until shaking completely stops.
  - Stay away from glass windows, heavy shelves, and exterior walls.
  - If outdoors, move to a clear, open area away from power lines, trees, and buildings.
- **After Shaking:**
  - Expect aftershocks.
  - Evacuate via pre-determined safe routes to open assembly areas.
  - Do not re-enter damaged buildings to retrieve belongings before professional structural safety inspection.
  - Rely exclusively on official PHIVOLCS announcements. Social media claims stating an exact time or magnitude of future earthquakes are unverified rumors.

### B. Landslides & Unstable Slopes
- Heavy rain following earthquakes causes rapid slope failures.
- Watch for warning signs: tension cracks in soil, leaning trees/poles, sudden muddy stream discharge, rumbling sounds.
- Never attempt to cross fresh landslide debris; secondary collapses can occur without warning.

### C. Tsunami Natural Warning Signs (Tsunami Safety and Preparedness)
- If near the coast and you experience:
  1. **Strong Ground Shaking** (difficulty standing),
  2. **Unusual Sea-Level Behavior** (sudden rapid withdrawal/retreat of the sea, exposing the seafloor, or abnormal rise),
  3. **Loud Roaring Sound** (resembling a train or low-flying aircraft from the ocean),
- **Rule:** Do NOT wait for an official siren or advisory. Move immediately to high ground (at least 10 meters above sea level) or inland. Never gather at the beach to watch retreating water.

### D. Volcanic Hazards & Lahars
- **Volcano Alert Levels (0–5):**
  - Respect Permanent Danger Zones (PDZ) and Extended Danger Zones (EDZ).
- **Ashfall Protection:**
  - Stay indoors; seal doors and windows with damp cloths.
  - Wear an N95 or damp cloth mask over nose and mouth, and protective goggles.
  - Avoid unnecessary vehicular travel as ash causes loss of traction and engine failure.
- **Lahars:**
  - Fast-moving mixtures of volcanic debris, ash, and water triggered by heavy rain.
  - Stay away from active river channels and gullies originating from the volcano.

## 4. Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP)
- **Fire Safety & SafeHome Escape Protocol:**
  - **Raise the Alarm:** Shout fire, trigger fire alarms, and inform everyone immediately.
  - **Evacuate:** Use safe, unobstructed exits. Stay low under smoke (smoke and toxic gases rise).
  - **Never Re-Enter:** Never return inside a burning or smoke-filled building for phones, pets, or valuables.
  - **Accountability:** Assemble at designated assembly points and report missing persons to arriving BFP firefighters rather than attempting dangerous untrained rescue.
